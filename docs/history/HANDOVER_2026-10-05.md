## 1. The aim, and how it evolved

### 1.1 Original aim (project description, still written in the Claude Project)

> Develop an algorithm that identifies favorable times to enter a **long** position in a stock (SPY) and determines corresponding **Stop Loss (SL) and Take Profit (TP)** levels, such that **TP is reached substantially more often than SL**, while remaining robust and realistic under out-of-sample evaluation. Thesis-quality, reproducible, honest record.

The original hypothesis (design document `SO_Design_Document.docx`, previous workspace): technical indicators plus qualitative reports are significantly related to SPY's price direction at the 1-minute level. The qualitative (text) half is **deferred** until the quantitative part is finished.

### 1.2 The success definition agreed in this workspace

- **2026-10-01 (design discussion):** success = a **higher total return than buy-and-hold over the same period, after costs** (no tax applies: Nicolas is in Qatar). Sharpe ratio and maximum drawdown are reported alongside.
- **2026-10-04 (after v1 failed, Nicolas's words):** his intention is to beat "the simple buy-and-hold with an arbitrary exit" long-term investing strategy using a "**buy based on information and exit based on a calculated risk tolerance**" strategy.
- **2026-10-04, success criteria (decided by Nicolas):**
  - **Primary:** total return after costs **above buy-and-hold** (chained over the out-of-sample windows).
  - **Secondary:** a **higher Sharpe ratio and a smaller maximum drawdown**, counted only if the return is **at least 90% of buy-and-hold's** ("comparable return").
  - The primary criterion stays the main one.

### 1.3 Alignment question for you

The project description still says "TP reached substantially more often than SL". The working criterion is "total return above buy-and-hold". These are **not the same**:
- a high TP rate is easy to obtain without any skill (e.g. a TP closer than the SL, or simply riding SPY's upward drift with symmetric barriers — v1 showed exactly this: at 0.25–0.9% distances, being long already beat the break-even TP rate);
- a high TP rate does not imply beating buy-and-hold, because capping winners at +δ gives up the large up-moves (v1 again).

Please check whether the experiments (v1, v2, step 13) are still pointed at what Nicolas actually wants, and say so explicitly if the project description should be updated or if the work has drifted from it. The previous assistant's view: the total-return criterion is the correct and stricter one, and the TP-rate formulation should be treated as a means, not the aim — but this was never formally written into the project description.

### 4.4 Execution and cost rules (shared by v1 and v2)

- Decision on the feature row of bar t (only bars ≤ t); entry at the **open of bar t+1**. v1: first entry 10:00 (decision 09:59), last 15:59; no overnight carry of a signal. v2: one decision per day at 15:58, fill at the 15:59 open.
- Costs: entry +$0.01/share; market exits (SL, stop, time limit, end) −$0.01/share; TP limit exits no slippage but only filled if price trades at least $0.01 beyond TP; IBKR fixed fees $0.005/share, min $1, max 1% per side. Sensitivity $0.00 / $0.01 / $0.02 on test. **These cost figures are informal assumptions** (trading guides, broker page, forum threads), not measurements.
- A bar that opens beyond a level exits at that bar's open (gap); otherwise at the level. SL wins if both are hit on one bar. SL/TP active from the bar after entry.
- Account compounds, fully invested per trade, one position at a time, cash earns 0%, **dividends ignored** (favours any strategy that spends time in cash).

## 5. Experiment v1 — minute entries with symmetric SL/TP (stopped)

**Protocol:** `docs/RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md` (version 1, 2026-10-01). Trial-log names: `signal_check_v1(_clean)`, `lgbm_ev_policy_v1(_clean)`.

**Design.**
- Target (TSBAR): for each decision minute and each distance d in 32 values (0.05%–2.00%; 21 training values 0.10%–1.00%), the outcome of a long trade with TP = open·(1+d), SL = open·(1−d) (rr = 1), at most **10 trading days** (time-limit exit at the close of day 10). Cell = `exit_bar_count|exit_price|exit_reason` (TP/SL/TL/NA).
- Features: TSIND (43), TSSEG, TSCTX; absolute-dollar features excluded (they encode the price level, i.e. the year).
- Model: one LightGBM P(TP) classifier per distance; policy: at each eligible minute compute the expected net return per distance from P(TP) (TL counted as a loss), take the best distance, buy if above a threshold ∈ {0, 0.02%, 0.05%, 0.10%}. This output format was a **proposal, never formally agreed**.
- Sampling every 15 minutes for training, uniqueness weights; validation/test simulate every minute.
- Walk-forward: 3-month validation and test windows, 10-session embargo, training window L ∈ {3, 5, 10} years, ~45 folds (first test ≈ 2015-05), selection on single-quarter validation total return.
- Baselines: buy-and-hold, random entry (20 runs, matched activity), fixed-time entry.
- **Stopping rule (signal check, step 07):** per (feature, distance, 10 quantile bins), a block-bootstrap (weeks) interval of the TP rate; a signal = lower bound above the break-even TP rate on training AND on validation with the same bin edges.

**Results (corrected data, 2026-10-05; uncorrected numbers identical or close, see `RESULTS_LOG.md`).**
- **Signal check: STOP.** 0 of 5,691 bins passed both windows. 1,339 training passes vs 142 expected by chance, almost all at distances where simply being long beat break-even (market drift). Only 19 bins beat the unconditional base rate.
- **Walk-forward, validation only (45 quarters, 2015–2026, 540 trials):** best of 12 settings chained **+158% vs +230%** for buy-and-hold over the same spans; at most 16 of 45 quarters beat buy-and-hold; invested 76–95% of the time. (Uncorrected: +162% vs +247%, ≤ 18 of 45.)
- Note: v1's buy-and-hold is measured to the strategy's last exit, so its value differs slightly per setting.

**Why it failed (interpretation).** A long-only, unleveraged strategy cannot hold more than 100% of SPY. Symmetric barriers cap winners at +δ, so even a strategy that is invested most of the time gives up the large up-moves. The only way to beat buy-and-hold is to be **out during declines and back in before the recovery**. That reasoning motivated v2.

## 6. Experiment v2 — trailing stop + model re-entry (stopped)

**Protocol:** `docs/RESEARCH_PROTOCOL_v2.md` (2.0 → 2.0.2). Trial-log names: `stop_reentry_v2` (uncorrected + a mislabeled duplicate, §8), `stop_reentry_v2_clean`.

**Design (all rules agreed before any v2 result).**
- **Invested by default.** Exit on an **intraday trailing stop**: stop = max(previous stop, highest minute close since entry × (1 − k·σ)), σ = 20-day daily-return volatility, k ∈ {3, 4, 5}.
- **Re-entry on information:** daily at 15:58 while in cash, a logistic regression (L2, C = 0.1) gives P(net 20-session forward return > 0); buy at the 15:59 open if P ≥ training base rate + offset, offset ∈ {−0.05, 0, +0.05}. **Forced re-entry after 60 cash decisions.** LightGBM (depth 2) fitted as a diagnostic only.
- 16 scale-free daily features (past returns 5–250d, MA50/MA200 distance, volatility and its ratio, distances from 60/250-day highs and the ATH, previous-day return/range, intraday return, sessions since the 60-day high).
- Label: buy at the 15:59 open, sell at the close 20 sessions later, net of costs > 0. Base rate 65.7% overall (34.8% in 2008 … 81.3% in 2017).
- Walk-forward: 44 folds, **20-session embargo**, training windows 10 years or all history, 18 candidates per fold, **selection on the mean validation excess return of the last 4 quarters (pooled)**. Each 3-month window starts invested (state reset).
- Baselines: buy-and-hold, same stop + fixed-delay re-entry (1/5/20), same stop + random re-entry (20 runs), 200-session moving-average rule.
- **Key identity:** an out-of-market episode that sells at S and buys back at R ends with S/R times the shares. The strategy wins only if, on average, it **buys back lower** than it sold. Averages of S/R − 1 are biased upward (Jensen), so the mean **log** share gain is used, compared with a drift reference.
- Stopping rules: (1) daily signal check (two-sided, vs the window's base rate, 5 quantile bins, monthly block bootstrap; training window 2015-03-20 → 2025-03-19, pooled validation of folds 40–43); (2) mechanism check on 2005–2014 (exploration); (3) walk-forward.

**Results (corrected data, 2026-10-05).**
- **Mechanism check (2005–2014):** every stop + fixed-delay variant lost to buy-and-hold (+69.0%), by −6.1 pts (k = 4, 5-day delay: +62.9%, Sharpe 0.37 vs 0.36, drawdown −41.5% vs −56.4%) to −100 pts. That best variant is isolated (neighbours far worse) and uses no information. **Post-exit path:** re-entering n decisions after a stop gives a negative mean log share gain at every n shown (1–60) and every k, below the drift reference except at n = 40; only 30–47% of stops would have been bought back lower. **Stops sell near the low of short dips that revert.** Oracle +1,086% to +1,423% (uninformative ceiling). MA200 rule +50.4% (drawdown −23.3%), 73% of buy-and-hold's return.
- **Daily signal check: STOP.** 0 of 80 tests passed training (4 expected by chance), 0 signals. Largest lift +8 pts with an interval including the base rate.
- **Walk-forward (validation only, 44 quarters 2015-04 → 2026-04, 792 trials):** all 18 candidates lost: chained +54% to +230% vs **+243%**; 14–21 of 44 quarters won. Honest pseudo out-of-sample (select on the 4 previous quarters, evaluate on the next): **+112% vs +237%**, 20 of 43 quarters. "Insurance pattern": the all-history candidates gain +2.6 to +5.1 pts in the 12 down quarters but lose −1.3 to −3.5 pts in the 32 up quarters. Mean validation AUC: logistic 0.52 (10y) / 0.47 (all), LightGBM 0.51 — no ranking ability.
- Uncorrected run: same verdicts (selection +141% vs +237%).

**Conclusion recorded:** v2 stopped under its protocol; confirmed on corrected data.

## 7. Exploration — "sell at strength" (step 13)

**Idea (Nicolas, 2026-10-04):** since selling on weakness (stops) is costly, sell when SPY is at or near its all-time high and buy back lower.

**Scope:** exploration only, data cut at 2015-03-18, events 2005–2014. ATH = highest high since 2005-01-03 (2005–2006 highs are pseudo-ATHs). 15 rules per run: sell when the 15:58 close is within 0.5% / 1% / 2% of the ATH; buy back after 5 / 20 / 60 decisions or after a 2% / 5% dip; forced after 60.

**Results on corrected data:**
- Forward returns after near-ATH days: slightly negative for 1–20 sessions at the tight thresholds (−0.02% to −0.14%) vs +0.02% to +0.43% for all days; **no difference significant**; positive at 40–60 sessions.
- **5 of 15 rules beat buy-and-hold:** 4 of 5 at the 0.5% threshold (best: 0.5% + 5% dip, +80.7% vs +69.0%, +11.7 pts), **none at 1%** (all lose), 1 at 2%. Drawdown essentially unchanged (−52% to −56%). About 15 independent episodes; most buy-backs were forced, not chosen.
- **On uncorrected data the same exploration said 14 of 15 rules lost.** The fake 2007 highs had removed most 2005–2007 near-ATH days. This is the clearest example in the project of a data error flipping a conclusion.

**Recorded conclusion:** weak and inconsistent, not evidence of an edge; **30 exploration trials** spent. If pursued, the rule must be fixed in a protocol before any 2015–2026 data is used, and choosing the 0.5% / 5% rule because it won here would be selection on exploration results. Whether to fold it into v3 is an **open decision** (§12).

## 10. Lessons learned

**About the market (evidence from this project, SPY 2005–2026):**
1. **Drift dominates.** About 66% of 20-session periods are positive; 2015–2026 validation buy-and-hold is +243%. Any test that compares a feature with break-even rather than with the **base rate** will mistake drift for signal (v1's signal check passed 1,339 bins on training for that reason; v2's check compares with the base rate and is two-sided).
2. **Capping upside loses.** Symmetric SL/TP gives up the big up-moves (v1).
3. **Selling on weakness loses.** Volatility stops tend to fire near the bottom of short dips that revert; buying back later means buying higher (v2 post-exit path).
4. **Selling at strength is, at best, unproven.** Mixed, non-monotonic exploration results (step 13).
5. **Single daily price-derived features do not reliably shift the 20-session up-rate**, and the re-entry model's AUC is ~0.5.
6. The only consistently favourable trait seen is **insurance**: models that sit out more do better in down quarters, but up quarters are 2.7 times as frequent, so the net effect is negative.

**About method:**
7. Use the mean **log** share gain, not the mean of S/R − 1 (Jensen bias), and compare with a drift reference.
8. Bootstrap intervals on bins concentrated in a few months can be degenerate ([1.0, 1.0]): require a minimum number of months per bin (`min_month_count_in`, e.g. 6) in new experiments.
9. The best candidate picked after the fact is optimistic; report the **prior-only selection** (pseudo out-of-sample) as the honest number.
10. Single-quarter validation selection is noisy; pooling 4 quarters helps but does not remove it.
11. Data errors can **reverse** a conclusion (step 13). A sanity check that only tests "high ≥ max(open, close)" misses wick outliers; check against neighbours too.
12. Cumulative features (`cum_max`, ATH distance) spread a single bad tick over years of data.

**About process:**
13. **Restart notebook kernels** after changing a `.py` file ("Run All" keeps old imports): this caused a mislabeled duplicate run.
14. **Tag the repository** before reruns; executed notebooks committed with outputs are the evidence.
15. Write verification scripts (dry run by default, logs in `records/`) for any bulk data change, and verify the result against a full rebuild.
16. Never edit the trial log; document anomalies in `RESULTS_LOG.md`.

## 11. Known limitations, open issues, and things that may have been missed

**Limitations already documented:**
- **Dividends ignored** in both strategy and benchmark: favours strategies that spend time in cash (buy-and-hold loses ~1.2–2%/year of yield). **Cash earns 0%**: disfavours them (4–5%/year in 2023–2025). These two partly offset but were never quantified.
- **Unadjusted prices:** ex-dividend gaps look like market moves. They can trigger v1's small SL distances on overnight holds and appear in v2's returns; impact not measured.
- **Costs are assumptions**, not measured fills; no event-day slippage.
- **One instrument (SPY), one period:** few bear markets (2008, 2011, 2015–16, 2018, 2020, 2022) → low statistical power.
- **ATH defined from 2005**, not SPY's true history.
- v2 state reset to invested at the start of every 3-month window; v2 model trained on all days but used only on cash days (distribution shift).
- 20-session labels overlap heavily (effective sample ≈ 125 independent periods per 10 years).

**Open technical issues:**
